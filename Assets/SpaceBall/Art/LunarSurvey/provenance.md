# Lunar survey surface

Source: [NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/), accessed 2026-11-05.

Credit: **NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio**. Visualization: Ernie Wright (USRA). Scientist: Noah Petro (NASA/GSFC). Source instruments: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC) and Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA). These are scientific Moon source textures, not AI-generated illustrations or a gameplay screenshot.

- `Moon_Albedo_2k.jpg` is the unmodified 2048×1024 `lroc_color_2k.jpg` from the 2025 color map.
- `Source~/ldem_4.tif` is NASA's 1440×720 floating-point elevation in kilometers relative to 1737.4 km. The `Source~` folder is ignored by Unity.
- `Moon_Height_RG16.png` is a 720×360 elevation map derived by Lanczos downsampling the DEM. Pixel uint16 = R×256+G; elevation km = uint16/65535×24−12. B is zero. The first/last rows are averaged to make each geometric pole a single point. It must be imported **linear, readable, uncompressed**, with U Repeat and V Clamp.
- `Moon_Normal_LOLA.png` is a 1440×720 tangent-space normal generated from the DEM's angular elevation derivatives, accounting for spherical longitude distance. Relief multiplier is 2. Normals fade to radial in a three-degree polar band to stabilize the longitude singularity. Import as **Normal map** without green-channel flipping, with U Repeat and V Clamp.

Both source maps are equirectangular, centered on longitude 0°. North is at the top of the file and at texture v=1. The code maps u=0.5, v=0.5 to local +X, local north to +Y, and increasing longitude toward +Z. Normal X points east; normal Y points north. No terrain is inferred from albedo shading.

`OrbitalLunarSurface.CreateMesh(height, radius)` creates 128×80 segments (10,449 vertices, 20,224 triangles). Height displacement is 2× the real elevation-to-lunar-radius ratio. Its reference radius is reduced so even the maximum encoded elevation remains 0.2% inside the supplied gameplay collision radius. Geometry is decorative only and has no collider.

The mesh uses radial vertex normals and an east/north tangent frame; the complete DEM normal texture supplies terrain slopes. This prevents counting terrain slopes once in vertex normals and a second time in a full-height normal map. The normal texture is therefore part of the intended material, not an optional decoration.

Rebuild with `python prepare_lunar_surface.py` (Pillow + numpy). `preparation-report.json` records exact dimensions, ranges, SHA-256 hashes and source URLs. NASA's [image use guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) apply; NASA does not endorse this game.

## Terrain ambient occlusion estimate

`Moon_AO_LOLA.png` is a 720×360 RGB texture generated by `python prepare_lunar_ao.py` from the existing NASA `Source~/ldem_4.tif`. All three channels contain the same gray value, allowing URP's green-channel and glTF's red-channel occlusion sampling to use the same resource. Import as a **linear Default texture**, not sRGB, with U Repeat, V Clamp and no power-of-two resizing. No albedo image is read, changed, or used as height; no directional light, day/night boundary, artificial structure, or scene shadow is baked into it.

The bake uses the same reference radius (1737.4 km), **2× elevation**, east/north normal frame and three-degree polar stabilization as the existing LOLA normal texture. For each output texel it samples **24 azimuths × 10 great-circle distances** (7.5, 15, 22.5, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, 180 and 240 km), or 240 terrain samples per texel. Geographic destinations use spherical great-circle equations; source lookup is bilinear, with longitude repeating and latitude clamped. For an angular distance δ and physical radii `r0=1737.4+2h0`, `rj=1737.4+2hj`, the terrain horizon elevation is:

`β(δ) = atan2(rj*cos(δ) − r0, rj*sin(δ))`

The maximum β along each azimuth defines its horizon. This radial/tangential projection includes sphere curvature: a flat sphere has β = −δ/2 and does not incorrectly occlude itself. Local DEM slopes supply the surface normal. For each azimuth, cosine-weighted incoming visibility integrates `(nt*cos(b)+nu*sin(b))*cos(b)` from the larger of the terrain horizon, local-normal horizon and zero elevation up to π/2. Dividing the summed visible integrals by the corresponding unblocked local-normal integrals produces `rawAO`. This radial-hemisphere approximation avoids counting an ordinary local slope as occlusion by itself; it is **not a physically exact full-hemisphere or multiple-scattering solution**.

The deliberately restrained artistic remap is `AO=clamp(1−0.55*sqrt(1−rawAO), 0.78, 1)`. It exposes weak crater/basin occlusion while retaining bright terrain. Within three degrees of either pole, longitude variation fades toward that latitude's row mean; each terminal row is constant. After 8-bit quantization, the range is **0.7804–1.0000**, the lowest-one-percent threshold is **0.8863**, the mean is **0.9719**, and **10.526%** of pixels are fully white. Only two of 259,200 pixels reach the configured floor. These statistics and exact source/output hashes are recorded in `ao-preparation-report.json`.

This is a finite-resolution artistic terrain AO resource. It does not include features below the DEM grid or occlusion from the reactor band and other game objects. Its generation and numeric checks do not establish correct native rendering or material synchronization in PICO Shared Space.
